Who benefits

1. Fraud victims who struggle with an English form: elders, first-time smartphone users, people who think in Hindi, Tamil or Bengali, and shopkeepers hit by fake payment screenshots and QR swaps. In 2025 alone, Indians filed 24 lakh financial fraud complaints on the national portal and lost ₹22,495 crore. Senior citizens made only about 1 lakh of those complaints but lost ₹4,005 crore, close to a fifth of all the money lost.
2. Banks and the 1930 helpline. A Turant report arrives complete, with the UTR, amount, time and the scammer's UPI ID. Operators stop pulling details out of a crying caller, and banks can act on the first call.
3. Cyber police cells, who today chase victims for missing details days later.
4. Families. Today only the educated grandchild "can do the form". With Turant, the person who lost the money reports it themselves.

Before and after

Before: Kamala, 67, a retired teacher in Madurai, gets a call at 6:40 pm from "SBI KYC department". She shares an OTP and ₹38,000 is gone. She calls her son in Bengaluru, who is in a meeting. At 10 pm he opens cybercrime.gov.in, but the form needs the transaction number, and she cannot find the right SMS among fifty bank messages. The complaint is filed at 9 am the next day, more than 14 hours later. By then the money has passed through four mule accounts and been withdrawn. Nothing can be frozen.

After: At 6:50 pm Kamala presses the mic and speaks in Tamil for forty seconds. Turant catches the amount, the time and the "KYC call", and asks her to read the bank SMS aloud, which gives it the UTR. It reads everything back in Tamil, she corrects one digit by voice, and at 6:58 pm she calls 1930 with a four-line script on screen. The bank marks the money still sitting in the first mule account. Time to report drops from over 14 hours to under 20 minutes.

Why speed is the whole game: the national fraud reporting system behind 1930 had blocked or put on hold more than ₹11,158 crore across 32.8 lakh complaints by June 2026. It only works if the report reaches the bank before the money is withdrawn, which police call the golden hour. In Bihar, registering one 1930 complaint by hand takes 16 to 18 minutes. In February 2026 the Home Minister warned states that unanswered 1930 calls cost victims their money, and in June he ordered the helpline revamped with AI and multiple languages. Turant puts that idea directly in the victim's hands.

How voice changes access, productivity and participation

Access: 98% of India's 886 million internet users use the internet in Indian languages, and 1 in 5 already use voice commands, more than half of them in rural India (IAMAI-Kantar 2024). People are ready for voice; the complaint process is not. With Turant, reporting no longer depends on reading English or typing fast.
Productivity: the portal needs a 200-character description, the bank name, UTR, date and amount. Turant assembles all of it from a few minutes of speech, and 1930 calls get shorter because the caller reads from a ready script.
Participation: many victims never report at all because the process feels impossible. Every extra report is data that helps police spot mule accounts and scam patterns earlier.

What could slow adoption, and how we would handle it

1. Nobody installs an app before they are scammed. So Turant is a web link, not an app store download, distributed where victims already are: an SMS link when someone calls 1930, a "Report fraud by voice" button inside bank and UPI apps, and QR posters at bank branches, post offices and ration shops. A version for keypad phones can run the same flow over an IVR number.
2. Trust, and scammers making fake copies. Turant needs no login and never asks for an OTP, PIN or money, and it says so out loud at the start. Ideally it is hosted on an official I4C or bank domain.
3. Speech accuracy for dialects and long numbers. Numbers are always read back digit by digit, typing is always available, and the speech layer can use Bhashini, the government platform that already offers speech recognition and synthesis in 22 Indian languages.
4. Privacy. Everything stays on the phone until the person taps Share or Call. Account numbers show only the last four digits.
5. Integration. Today Turant prepares the complaint. The bigger impact comes from a pilot with one state cyber cell or one bank where a Turant report is pushed straight into the existing fraud reporting system, so the freeze request starts the moment the person finishes speaking.
6. Fake or duplicate complaints. Turant only structures what the person says; official verification by banks and police stays unchanged.

How we would know it is working: the median time from fraud to report, the share of reports filed within one hour, the share that include a UTR, the money frozen per complaint, and the share of reports made in a language other than English.

UPI handled 25 billion transactions in October 2026 alone. If digital payments are going to reach the next few hundred million Indians, the safety net has to speak their language too. Turant makes "I've been cheated" something anyone can say, and have acted on, while there is still time to get the money back.
