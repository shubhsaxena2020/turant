Who benefits, and roughly how many: People who lose money to online payment fraud and struggle with an English form. In 2025 Indians filed over 24 lakh financial fraud complaints and lost ₹22,495 crore (MHA, Rajya Sabha). Senior citizens filed about 1 lakh of those complaints but lost ₹4,005 crore, close to a fifth of all money lost. Turant is for them: elders, first-time smartphone users, people who think in Hindi, Tamil or Bengali, and shopkeepers hit by fake payment screenshots. It also helps 1930 operators and banks, who get complete complaints instead of chasing missing details.

What changes: Speed is everything, and today the process is slow. The national reporting system behind 1930 has blocked or held over ₹11,158 crore across 32.8 lakh complaints, but only when the report reaches the bank before the money is withdrawn. In Bihar, registering one 1930 complaint by hand takes 16 to 18 minutes.

Before: Kamala, 67, a retired teacher in Madurai, shares an OTP on a fake "SBI KYC" call at 6:40 pm and loses ₹38,000. She cannot read the English portal, cannot find the transaction number among fifty SMSes, and waits for her son. The complaint is filed the next morning. The money is gone.

After: at 6:50 pm she presses one button and speaks in Tamil for forty seconds. Turant catches the amount, time and scam type, asks her to read the bank SMS aloud to get the UTR, reads everything back, and at 6:58 pm she calls 1930 with four lines on screen to say. Time to report drops from over 14 hours to under 20 minutes, and she did it herself, in her own language.

How we would know it worked (targets for a 3-month pilot with one state cyber cell or one bank):
1. Median time from fraud to a complete report under 1 hour.
2. At least 70% of Turant reports include the UTR, the field banks need to freeze money.
3. At least 40% of reports made in a language other than English.
4. Report completion rate above 80% for users over 60.
5. More money held per complaint than the pilot area's current baseline.

The main risk: speech recognition can mishear long numbers, dialects and noisy speech, and a wrong UTR or amount in a fraud complaint is worse than none. Turant reduces this by reading every number back in digit groups for a spoken "yes", switching to a big keypad after two misses, and letting people read the bank SMS aloud instead of dictating digits. Accuracy for less common languages must still be tested with real users before scale. A second risk is that people only look for help after being cheated, so Turant has to be reachable from where victims already are: an SMS link from 1930, a button inside bank and UPI apps, and posters at bank branches.
