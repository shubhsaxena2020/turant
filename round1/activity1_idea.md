The problem: Reporting an online payment fraud in time. When someone loses money to a UPI scam, a fake KYC call or a "sent you money by mistake" trick, they have to file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930, fast, with exact transaction details.

Who has it, where and how often: Anyone with a UPI app, in every city and village. In 2025 Indians filed over 24 lakh financial fraud complaints, over 6,500 every day. A kirana owner in Patna, a retired teacher in Madurai, a delivery rider in Indore. The people scammers target most are elders, first-time smartphone users and people who are not comfortable reading or typing English. The portal form is long and mostly in English. It asks for a 12-digit UTR number, the scammer's UPI ID, the bank, the exact date and time, and more. A panicked person has to dig these out of three different apps and type them on a small keyboard. The 1930 line is often busy. Meanwhile the money is moving. Scammers split it across mule accounts within hours, and a bank can only freeze what has not moved yet. So many victims give up, or wait for a son or granddaughter to fill the form the next day, when it is already too late.

The voice-first product, step by step:
1. Open Turant and press one big mic button. Just say what happened, in any language, even mixed: "Mere account se 4,999 rupaye kat gaye, ek aadmi ne bola KYC update karna hai, das minute pehle."
2. Turant listens and pulls out the facts on its own: amount, time, type of scam, which app.
3. It asks only for what is still missing, one spoken question at a time. "Which bank was the money taken from?" "Can you read me the 12-digit number from the payment SMS?" You can simply read the SMS out loud and it finds the number.
4. It reads the full complaint back in your language and asks "Is this right?" You fix mistakes by speaking: "No, the amount was 5,999."
5. It hands you everything ready to go: a one-tap call to 1930 with a short script to read out, the complaint text formatted for the portal, and a message for your bank. If the network drops, it saves everything on the phone and finishes when you are back online.
6. It ends with spoken next steps: block your card, never share an OTP, keep your screenshots.

Why voice comes first: In a panic, people can talk but they cannot fill forms. Speaking in your own language removes the English barrier and the typing barrier at the exact moment speed matters most. The person who actually knows what happened can report it themselves, in minutes, without waiting for someone else. Every minute saved is money that can still be frozen before it disappears.
